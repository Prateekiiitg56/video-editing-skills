# Style: Documentary

Load with: `editing-foundations`, `captions-typography` (sentence style), `sound-design`, `color-grading`.

## Signature

- Story comes from the people: interview answers carry the narrative, B-roll shows what they describe.
- Restraint: cuts motivated by content, longer shots (4-10 s), few or no effects.
- J/L cuts constantly; natural sound (nat-sound) breaks between interview bites.
- Lower thirds identify speakers on first appearance.
- Captions: sentence case, 1-2 lines, bottom-centre (or none for long-form with good audio).
- Score: understated, supports emotion; music changes mark chapter shifts.

## Workflow (paper edit)

1. Transcribe all interviews (word-level, speaker labels).
2. Pull selects: the strongest sentences (emotion, clarity, surprise) into a selects doc with timecodes.
3. Paper edit: arrange selects into a structure (thesis → context → conflict → turn → resolution) before touching the timeline.
4. Radio cut: assemble audio only and listen to the story logic (is it understandable without picture?).
5. Cover with B-roll: every bite gets relevant B-roll except moments where the face matters (emotion, credibility).
6. Add nat-sound pops (0.5-2 s of real sound up full) between sections.
7. Archival/stills: slow push/pan (Ken Burns) 3-6 s, eased, toward the important detail.
8. Grade for consistency; protect skin; keep it natural.

## Interview framing on screen

- Speaker off-centre, looking into the open side of the frame.
- Two-camera interviews: cut to the second angle to hide edits in the answer.
- Lower third: name (medium weight) + role (light), 3-5 s, slide/fade 300 ms, left-aligned inside safe zone.

## Ethics

- Never cut a bite so it changes the speaker's meaning.
- Mark recreated/staged scenes when it matters to the story.
- Credit archival footage and music.
